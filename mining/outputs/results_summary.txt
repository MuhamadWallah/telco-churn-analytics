9. What the results mean: explanation, possible solutions and recommendation
==============================================================================
Built by rules from the numbers above (mining/insights.py); the what-if simulation refits
Gradient Boosting once per cross-validation fold.

9.1 In short
~~~~~~~~~~~~
  - Best model: Gradient Boosting (5-fold cross-validated ROC-AUC 0.847): it
    gives a random churner a higher risk score than a random non-churner 85%
    of the time.
  - Strongest churn signal: Contract type - 'Month-to-month' customers churn
    at 42.7% against 2.8% for 'Two year'.
  - Recommended solution: Contract-upgrade offer, expected to prevent about
    406 churners among 2,220 active customers (54% fewer).
  - Decision threshold: 0.15 instead of 0.5 - catches 90% of churners instead
    of 52%, at 39% lower error cost (or 0.35 if the campaign must be smaller).
  - Who to contact first: the 488 active customers in the High risk band.

The sections below explain each point in full, with the numbers behind it.

9.2 What the model results say
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
The warehouse holds 7,043 customers, of whom 1,869 (26.54%) churned. A 'model'
that always answers 'will not churn' would already be 73.5% accurate while
catching no churner at all, so accuracy alone is misleading here. The measures
that matter are:

  - Recall - the share of real churners the model catches. A missed churner is
    a lost customer.
  - Precision - when the model flags a customer, how often that customer
    really churns. A false alarm wastes a retention offer.
  - F1-score - one number that balances precision and recall.
  - ROC-AUC - how well the model ranks customers by risk, whatever threshold
    is used: 0.5 is a coin toss and 1.0 is perfect. A value of 0.85 means that
    for a random churner and a random non-churner, the model gives the churner
    the higher score 85% of the time.

On the untouched test set (1,409 customers, 374 of them churners), at the
default threshold of 0.5:

  - Logistic Regression caught 209 of 374 churners (recall 55.9%) and missed
    165. It flagged 318 customers, of whom 209 really churned (precision
    65.7%); the other 109 would have received an unnecessary offer. Accuracy
    80.6%, F1 0.604, ROC-AUC 0.842.
  - Decision Tree caught 212 of 374 churners (recall 56.7%) and missed 162. It
    flagged 334 customers, of whom 212 really churned (precision 63.5%); the
    other 122 would have received an unnecessary offer. Accuracy 79.8%, F1
    0.599, ROC-AUC 0.828.
  - Random Forest caught 289 of 374 churners (recall 77.3%) and missed 85. It
    flagged 548 customers, of whom 289 really churned (precision 52.7%); the
    other 259 would have received an unnecessary offer. Accuracy 75.6%, F1
    0.627, ROC-AUC 0.843.
  - Gradient Boosting caught 195 of 374 churners (recall 52.1%) and missed
    179. It flagged 291 customers, of whom 195 really churned (precision
    67.0%); the other 96 would have received an unnecessary offer. Accuracy
    80.5%, F1 0.586, ROC-AUC 0.845.

In short, on this test set: Random Forest catches the most churners; Gradient
Boosting raises the fewest false alarms and ranks customers best. Which one to
use is explained next.

9.3 Which model to use, and why
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
One test split can be lucky or unlucky, so the models are compared on 5-fold
cross-validation: every customer is scored once by a model that did not see
that customer.

              model CV ROC-AUC (mean) std across folds test ROC-AUC CV recall (0.5)
  Gradient Boosting            0.8474           0.0108       0.8454          0.5281
      Random Forest            0.8458           0.0117       0.8428          0.7672
Logistic Regression            0.8451           0.0134       0.8421          0.5527
      Decision Tree            0.8310           0.0083       0.8276          0.5628

Use Gradient Boosting as the scoring model. Reasons:

  1. It has the highest mean cross-validated ROC-AUC (0.8474), so it ranks
     customers by risk best - and a ranked contact list is what a retention
     team works from.
  2. Its lead over Random Forest is only 0.0016, smaller than the fold-to-fold
     variation (0.0108). Random Forest and Logistic Regression are therefore
     practically tied with it; Decision Tree is clearly behind (0.0164 lower).
  3. Its test ROC-AUC (0.8454) is within 0.0020 of its cross-validation mean,
     so there is no sign that it over-fits the training data.
  4. Random Forest catches more churners at the default threshold (test recall
     77.3% against 52.1%), but that comes mainly from its class weighting,
     which works like a lower threshold, not from better ranking (ROC-AUC
     0.8428). With a tuned threshold of 0.35, Gradient Boosting catches 70.9%
     of churners too (next section).
  5. If the model must be easy to explain (for example to managers or a
     regulator), Logistic Regression is an acceptable alternative: it is the
     simplest of the tied models and loses only 0.0023 ROC-AUC.
  6. The results reproduce the earlier (MySQL-era) analysis: no metric differs
     by more than 0.0080.

9.4 Which decision threshold to use, and why
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
A customer is flagged when the predicted churn probability is at least the
threshold; 0.5 is only a default. Two alternative thresholds for Gradient
Boosting were chosen on the TRAINING data (out-of-fold predictions) and then
checked once on the untouched test set:

       rule threshold recall precision missed churners wasted offers flagged error cost
    default      0.50  52.1%     67.0%             179            96   20.7%        991
    best F1      0.35  70.9%     56.6%             109           203   33.2%        748
lowest cost      0.15  89.8%     45.0%              38           411   53.0%        601

Error cost = 5 x missed churners + 1 x wasted offers. The ratio 5 : 1 is an
illustrative assumption set in churn_mining.py; a real company should use its
own figures.

Recommended threshold: 0.15 (the lowest-cost rule). Reasons:

  1. It is the rule that matches the business goal - the fewest costly
     mistakes - and it was chosen on training data only, so the test result
     below is a fair check rather than a tuned score.
  2. The test set confirms it: error cost 601 against 991 at the default 0.5
     (39% lower) and 748 at the best-F1 threshold.
  3. It catches 89.8% of churners instead of 52.1%: 141 more churners reached
     in the test set.
  4. The price: more false alarms (411 instead of 96) and a larger campaign
     (53% of customers flagged). If the retention budget cannot cover that,
     use the best-F1 threshold 0.35: it flags 33% and still catches 70.9%.

9.7 Possible solutions
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Each finding was matched with retention actions. For actions that change
something the model can see, the model was asked a what-if question for every
customer who is still active: how much would this customer's churn probability
fall if the action were applied? Each customer is re-scored by the cross-
validation model that did not train on them. 'Expected churners' is the sum of
the probabilities. The numbers assume every targeted customer accepts, and the
target groups overlap, so the effects cannot be added up.

rank                           solution active customers targeted expected churners before -> after prevented per 100 contacted monthly revenue protected
   1             Contract-upgrade offer                     2,220                        749 -> 343       406              18.3                    27,237
   2 Free security & tech-support trial                     2,761                        769 -> 524       244               8.8                    17,752
   3        Automatic-payment incentive                     1,294                        439 -> 368        71               5.5                     5,405
   4        10% discount for high bills                     1,191                        286 -> 241        44               3.7                     4,057

  - Contract-upgrade offer (2,220 active customers on a month-to-month
    contract). Offer month-to-month customers a reward (for example a price
    lock or one free month) for moving to a one-year contract. Expected
    churners 749 -> 343 (406 prevented, 54% fewer). Trade-off: the reward
    costs money, and a contract only delays an unhappy customer, so it must go
    together with good service.
  - Free security & tech-support trial (2,761 active internet customers
    without online security or without tech support). Give internet customers
    who lack online security or tech support both services free for a trial
    period (for example three months). Expected churners 769 -> 524 (244
    prevented, 32% fewer). Trade-off: the services cost money to deliver
    during the trial, but can become extra revenue if customers keep them.
  - Automatic-payment incentive (1,294 active customers who pay by electronic
    check). Encourage electronic-check payers to move to automatic bank-
    transfer or credit-card payment (for example with a small one-off bill
    credit). Expected churners 439 -> 368 (71 prevented, 16% fewer). Trade-
    off: cheap to run, but the payment method may only be a sign of a less
    committed customer, so the true effect could be small.
  - 10% discount for high bills (1,191 active customers in the top 25% of
    monthly charges). Give the customers with the highest monthly bills (top
    25%) a 10% discount. Expected churners 286 -> 241 (44 prevented, 16%
    fewer). The discount costs about 9,227 a month on customers who would have
    stayed anyway, more than the 4,057 it protects: net -5,171 a month. For
    196 customers the model's probability goes UP after the change, a sign
    that it has not learned a reliable effect here. Trade-off: every targeted
    customer gets the discount, including the many who would have stayed
    anyway.

Supporting actions (not simulated)
----------------------------------

  - First-year onboarding programme (1,149 active customers with tenure of 12
    months or less, 400 expected churners, 344 in the High band). Welcome
    calls, a set-up check and reviews after 3 and 6 months for customers in
    their first year. Not simulated: tenure cannot be changed by an action, so
    the model cannot estimate the effect.
  - Fibre-optic service review (1,799 active fibre-optic customers, 552
    expected churners, 384 in the High band). Investigate fibre-optic quality
    and price (outages, speed, competitor offers) and fix the causes that are
    found. Not simulated: the action is an investigation; moving customers to
    DSL would not be a real solution.
  - Senior-customer care (666 active senior customers, 195 expected churners,
    132 in the High band). A dedicated support line and simpler plans for
    senior customers. Not simulated: age cannot be changed, and the data has
    no measure of care quality.

9.8 Recommended solution, and why it was chosen
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
Decision rule: among the solutions the model can simulate, recommend the one
that prevents the most expected churners, leaving out any solution whose known
cost is larger than the revenue it protects.

Recommended: Contract-upgrade offer. Offer month-to-month customers a reward
(for example a price lock or one free month) for moving to a one-year
contract.

Why this solution was chosen:

  1. Largest effect. The model expects it to prevent about 406 of the 749
     expected churners among its 2,220 target customers (54% fewer) - 161 more
     than the runner-up, Free security & tech-support trial (244).
  2. The data points the same way. 'Month-to-month' customers churn at 42.7%
     against 11.3% for 'One year' (contract type).
  3. The model relies on it. Contract type is number 1 of 19 columns in the
     permutation importance (test ROC-AUC drop 0.0639), 2.5 times the next
     column, so the what-if rests on the model's strongest evidence.
  4. Revenue. It protects about 27,237 of monthly revenue (the most of all
     solutions), so the offer pays for itself as long as it costs less than
     12.27 per targeted customer per month.
  5. Efficiency. It prevents 18.3 churners per 100 customers contacted, the
     highest of all solutions.
  6. Where to start. The 487 target customers in the High risk band account
     for 42% of the expected effect (169 churners) with 22% of the contacts.
     The full list, sorted by expected benefit, is saved in
     mining/outputs/recommended_action_targets.csv.

Second choice: Free security & tech-support trial (244 churners prevented).
1,638 customers are in both target groups, so the two actions together reach
3,343 active customers and can be combined in one campaign.

Trade-off of the recommended solution: the reward costs money, and a contract
only delays an unhappy customer, so it must go together with good service.

9.9 Limits and next steps
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
  - These are associations in one snapshot of a public sample, not causes.
    Customers who choose a longer contract or an add-on may differ in ways the
    data does not record, so the real effect of an offer is likely to be
    smaller than the what-if number.
  - The what-if assumes that every targeted customer accepts and that nothing
    else about the customer changes.
  - Verify before rolling out: run a controlled pilot. Randomly give the offer
    to half of the targeted customers, keep the other half as a control group,
    and compare churn after 3 to 6 months.
  - Check fairness before any automatic use: compare offers and error rates
    across groups such as senior citizens and gender.
  - Re-run churn_mining.py after every warehouse reload: this explanation is
    rebuilt from the new numbers automatically.
